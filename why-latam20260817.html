Why Latin America

01 / The bottleneck

Enrollment, not science, is what delays trials

Roughly 80% of clinical trials fail to meet their enrollment timelines, up to half of sites enroll one patient or none, and a delayed launch can cost $8M a day in lost revenue.1

02 / Patient access

The patients are there, and under-enrolled

The region holds more than 8% of the world's population but hosted about 3.6–5% of global oncology trials in 2023.2 That gap is the opportunity: 650M+ people, over 80% urban, with treatment-naïve populations.3

It is also among the most ancestrally admixed regions on the planet — Native American, European and African lineages within the same trial population, documented across five countries.8 Representativeness is structural here, not engineered site by site.

03 / Seasonal advantage

Two hemispheres, one shorter calendar

For influenza, RSV and other seasonal indications the Southern Hemisphere winter runs about six months offset from the Northern one, so a parallel cohort can enroll here while northern sites wait for the season.9

04 / Cost & proximity

Lower cost, same time zone

Sponsors in Brazil and Argentina typically pay 60–70% of equivalent U.S. costs,3 and the region shares U.S. time zones, so the savings don't cost real-time collaboration. Nor is it a shrinking market: regional trial spend is projected to grow from about $1.5B in 2025 to $2.8B by 2033, with Argentina growing fastest.4

05 / Medical devices

MedTech is where the region is professionalizing fastest

Latin America was 2.1% of the global medical device trials market in 2024 — about $357M, heading to $457M by 2030. Within it, pivotal studies are the largest segment at 42.4% and the fastest growing, with Brazil leading.5 Pivotal device work is what has to survive an FDA inspection — work we have already had inspected.9

06 / Regulatory momentum

The rules are getting faster, not slower

Brazil's Law 14.874/2024 gives the regulator 90 business days to respond and lets studies start on local ethics committee review alone.6 Decree 12.651/2025 then created the national research ethics system and set review timeframes.7 Every country in the region runs under ICH-GCP with active inspections,9 and the region already carries about 15.6% of global Phase III oncology trials.2

08 / Where friction shows up

Where it gets hard, and how we clear it

The table is also the honest version of the argument: on those medians, Peru or Colombia can take two to three times longer to activate than Mexico.2 Regulatory timelines and customs are where time is lost here, and neither is solved remotely. We run in-country regulatory staff and dedicated customs brokers, so the friction is absorbed before it reaches your timeline.9
